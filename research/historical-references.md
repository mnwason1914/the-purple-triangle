## 2. Fact cards

### 2.1 The 1933 ban, by state
- **Fact:** Bans came state by state: **Bavaria 13 Apr 1933** (after a *Crisis* booklet campaign), **Saxony 18 Apr**, **Thuringia 26 Apr**, **Baden 15 May**; others followed; **Prussia** (which includes Westphalia) banned shortly before 25 Jun 1933. Gestapo occupied the Magdeburg office/factory 24 Apr 1933 (returned 29 Apr). **28 Jun 1933**: 30 SA men occupied Magdeburg, closed the factory, hoisted the swastika; radio announced it 29 Jun. **21, 23, 24 Aug 1933**: 65,189 kg of books, Bibles and pictures burned on 25 trucks at Magdeburg's edge. Much literature had been moved and hidden beforehand. Berlin-Wilmersdorf convention **25 Jun 1933**: ~7,000 attended; ~2,100,000 copies of a "Declaration of Facts" distributed to officials → persecution wave.
- **Place/Year:** Reich-wide; Westphalia = Prussia, mid/late June 1933.
- **Source:** #1 (V).
- **Game use:** In a Westphalian town the ban arrives *later* than in Bavaria → spring 1933 chapter where news/rumor from the south precedes the local order; the family has weeks to decide what to hide. Prop: a crate of books quietly moved to a relative's barn before the police come. Background radio line about Magdeburg (29 Jun).

### 2.2 Secret meetings and contact networks
- **Fact:** Small home meetings continued despite risk. From 1934–35 the country was divided into **13 regions** each with a regional service director (often unknown to local members). On **7 Oct 1934** groups across Germany met at **9:00 a.m.**, prayed, and sent a letter to the government; in Mainz, Konrad Franke hosted in a one-room flat and invited the fearful to leave—none did. Hamburg: 142 arrested within days afterward. Memorial **17 Apr 1935**: a Gestapo circular (3 Apr) planned a raid; a friendly insider leaked it; groups met at varied times, used a shop as cover, or pretended to **play cards** (Franz Kohlhofer's group near Bamberg). Baptisms done secretly in rivers (Neisse 1936; Weser at Dehme 1941) or a **bathtub**.
- **Source:** #2 (V).
- **Game use:** "Card game" cover mechanic — when a stranger enters, the player must swap Bibles for playing cards in time. Meeting times vary week to week (schedule puzzle). A sympathetic official who leaks a circular = a morally ambiguous NPC.

### 2.3 Printing and smuggling literature
- **Fact:** Mimeographed *Wachtturm* produced in many cities (Munich 500 copies; Schwarzenberg; Hamburg coordinated with coded orders). Hamburg couple Brembach copied at night and hid the machine **in a potato bin**; a search missed it. Literature smuggled from Switzerland via Czechoslovakia over the **Riesengebirge**: teams crossed **at midnight, twice a week**, with scouts, flashlights and a **password changed weekly**; also French, Saar, Swiss, Dutch borders. Large depots (30–50 tons) were broken up; booklets left under doormats. Magazines mailed under disguised titles (e.g., "The Jonadab"), with handwritten, varying addresses. Ilse Unterdörfer rode a motorcycle/bicycle as courier (Chemnitz–Olbernhau). Wewelsburg prisoners later built a **wooden mimeograph**. Messages to sisters in **lemon-juice** invisible ink (Cyranek network). Charlotte Müller and sister Käthe distributed copies to addresses supplied by their father (Chemnitz until autumn 1935, then Beierfeld until Aug 1936).
- **Source:** #1, #2, #3, #7 (V).
- **Game use:** Hiding-place minigame (potato bin, coffee pot, schoolbag, pantry); courier route with a weekly password; invisible-ink letter you must heat over a candle to read.

### 2.4 Lucerne Resolution leaflet campaign — 12 December 1936
- **Fact:** Lucerne convention **4–7 Sep 1936**; a circular of 21 Aug 1936 ordered travellers stopped/passports seized; ~300 of 1,000+ planned German attendees got there. Resolution sent to Hitler's government and the Vatican (delivered 9 Sep 1936). **300,000** copies printed in **Bern**; 200,000 went via Prague across the border; 100,000 seized in the Netherlands. On **12 Dec 1936, from 5:00 to 7:00 p.m.**, about **3,450** Witnesses each distributed **20–40 copies**, placing them **in mailboxes or under doors — one per house, up to three in large buildings**. Police caught only **about a dozen** distributors. Police then went house to house asking; residents **denied receiving** leaflets, which confused the police. Context: mass arrests ("Gestapo strike") had begun **28 Aug 1936**; reported preaching Oct–Dec 1936: 3,600 workers.
- **Source:** #2 (V).
- **Game use:** Timed mission: two-hour window at winter dusk (sunset ~16:15 in December → already dark), a quota of ~20–40 leaflets, one per door; patrol avoidance; next-day scene where a policeman questions neighbors and one neighbor says "I got nothing" (neighbor-who-looks-away).

### 2.5 "Open Letter" campaign — 20 June 1937
- **Fact:** Written by Harbeck; named Gestapo officials and described ~18 killings (e.g., Peter Heinen, beaten to death, Gelsenkirchen, Oct 1936). Stencils went via Prague. On 20 Mar 1937 Unterdörfer and Frost were arrested in Berlin and the duplicator seized. Heinrich Dietschi organized distribution on **20 Jun 1937**; Strohmeyer and Kluckhuhn printed **≥69,000 copies in Lemgo** (Lippe, near Bad Lippspringe!) — each got 3 years then "protective custody". Aftermath arrests: Elfriede Löhr seized when picking up a **stew pot** at a watched meeting point; Dietschi arrested after Paris convention (4 yrs), his wife tortured in Dortmund's **Steinwache**.
- **Source:** #2 (V).
- **Game use:** Regional flavour — Lemgo is ~30 km from Bad Lippspringe; a fictional town in East Westphalia/Lippe can plausibly receive these copies. "Stew-pot pickup" as a trap scene.

### 2.6 Gestapo surveillance, house searches, interrogation
- **Fact:** Special Gestapo command (1936) built address files, partly from members' *Daily Heavenly Manna* birthday books; trained agents to imitate members, including praying/studying. House searches: Mrs. Poddig hid mail in the pantry; agent searched loosely and warned that children not in Hitler Youth would be taken. Mrs. Kornelius hid literature in a **coffee pot** and handed it to her sister; 5-year-old Siegfried Kornelius put literature in his brother's **schoolbag** and waited outside. Mrs. Strenge refused to open; police brought a **locksmith**. Kusserow house searched **~18 times 1933–45**. Interrogation: Georg Bär—nightly beatings in Dresden; interrogator showed lists of travelling ministers. Hisiger struck with a **revolver butt** for refusing to name others. Emil Wilde's wife died in Gestapo custody 1937 after beatings.
- **Source:** #1, #2, #4, #8 (V).
- **Game use:** Knock-at-the-door scene with a child-hiding option; "birthday book" as a dangerous object (an address list the family must decide to burn).

### 2.7 Informers and betrayal
- **Fact:** Berlin zoo meetings arranged by a chair-renter (Varduhn) exposed; Klohe used as bait; Hildegard Mesch signalled leader Winkler away but he was arrested at night anyway (Aug 1936). Hans Müller, a traitor, betrayed the Cyranek network (Cyranek executed Dresden 1941). Auguste Schneider arrested Sep 1937 after Gestapo *claimed* Wandres had identified her (a pressure tactic). Wandres evaded capture ~3.5 years because he had members destroy photos of him (Gestapo knew his name, not face); arrested Dresden 3 Sep 1937.
- **Source:** #2 (V).
- **Game use:** "They said your friend named you" — interrogation bluff mechanic. No photographs policy as a family rule.

### 2.8 The "Erklärung" (declaration) offered for release
- **Fact:** Local forms existed earlier; late 1938 Himmler ordered a uniform text; April 1939 directive required it for release. Uniform text (5 points): (1) the IBV teaches error and pursues anti-state aims under cover of religion; (2) I have left it completely; (3) I will never again take part, will **report** anyone who approaches me with its teaching, and **hand in** any literature sent to me at the nearest police station; (4) I will respect the laws, especially in wartime, **defend the Fatherland with weapon in hand**, and join the national community; (5) breaking this means immediate return to protective custody. Header: camp, Dept. II, name, birth date/place, signature, date. Few signed; signers were often mocked, made to walk an "honor walk", and many were drafted and died at the front. Some asked to annul signatures (after Dickmann's execution, two prisoners did). Release offers: Heinrich Kaufmann refused his house keys, food and his wife's release from Ravensbrück; Magdalena Kusserow (17) refused release offer in Vechta; Charlotte Müller refused after sentence → camp. Elsa Abt (nursing a baby, 1940) signed only a shortened statement that she knew the consequences, then called out to Harald that she had not compromised.
- **Source:** #2, #4, #6, #7, #15 (V); #16, #17 (S).
- **Game use:** The central moral choice: a paper on a table with five points; the player can read each clause (the "report others" clause is the hook). Elsa Abt-style nuance: a guard offers a watered-down wording.

### 2.9 Children: school, salute, removal
- **Fact:** Children refused flag salute, Nazi songs, "Heil Hitler"; Kusserow parents rehearsed answers at home. Spring 1939: Elisabeth (13), Hans-Werner (9), Paul-Gerhard (7) taken **from school** by police under court order → reform school **Dorsten**, then intercepted by Gestapo → Nazi school **Nettelstadt near Minden**; separated; mother smuggled letters and met them once. Helmut Knöller and Willi Seitz (Jan 1937) expelled for refusing salute. Appel family children taken July 1941; son Walter drafted at 17, refused uniform, executed. Total **860 children** taken from parents. Simone Arnold (Alsace) expelled, then 9 Jul 1943 taken to Wessenberg reform school in Constance; mother visited 13 times in 22 months.
- **Source:** #1, #2, #3, #4 (V); #14 (S).
- **Game use:** Classroom scene with the morning flag ritual; a teacher who mocks vs. one who quietly lets the child stand silent. Letter-smuggling to a child in a home.

### 2.10 Military refusal and executions
- **Fact:** August Dickmann (b. 1910, Dinslaken): in Sachsenhausen since 1937; after war began refused to sign induction slip; Himmler approved public execution; **15 Sep 1939** shot on the roll-call square before all inmates (~600 Witnesses forced to watch per Yearbook); commandant Baranowsky then offered the declaration over loudspeakers—instead two asked to annul earlier signatures. NYT reported it 17 Sep 1939. Plaque 18 Sep 1999. Brother Heinrich also in camp (Yearbook). Others: Wilhelm Kusserow shot Münster **27 Apr 1940** (inferred from letter dated 26 Apr), Wolfgang Kusserow beheaded Brandenburg **28 Mar 1942** (inferred from letter 27 Mar); Johannes Harms Nov 1940; Bühler and Ballreich shot Wiesbaden 25 Jun 1940. Totals per 1974 Yearbook: 253 sentenced to death, 203 executed; 2005 Watchtower: ~270 executed, ~1,200 died.
- **Source:** #2, #3, #4, #5, #10, #13 (V).
- **Game use:** Late-game shadow (1939+) beyond the 1933–37 core: an older son's draft notice arrives as an ending branch. Never stage a real execution; reference by letter only.

### 2.11 Camps, purple triangle, Bible study inside
- **Fact:** Witnesses were a separate category marked with the **purple (violet) triangle**; pre-war 5–10% of camp prisoners. New arrivals: 25 lashes; day began 4:30 a.m. Absolute mail ban March 1938 for 9 months, then **5 lines/month**. Sachsenhausen: daily text at breakfast, discussed in yard; three Watchtowers smuggled in a **wooden leg** (1939/40), read by flashlight; 1942 Sunday studies with 220–250 men, lookouts posted. Bible **baked into gingerbread**; ~150 letters smuggled out via Fritsche. Purple-triangle prisoners trusted as workers → housekeeping for SS families.
- **Source:** #2, #3, #6, #11 (V).
- **Game use:** Epilogue / letter-from-camp device limited to five lines — a writing mechanic with a character limit.

### 2.12 Women's experiences
- **Fact:** **Moringen** (from 1935; nearly half of known women Witnesses) → ~600 moved to **Lichtenburg** Dec 1937 (>40% Witnesses May 1939) → **Ravensbrück** opened 15 May 1939 (~950 women, ~400 Witnesses). At Lichtenburg Witnesses refused a patriotic radio ceremony and were **fire-hosed**; refused to decorate for Hitler's birthday → 3 weeks dark cells. Charlotte Müller refused to collect swastika flags in laundry → penal block ~1 year. Women flogged (25 strokes) after Himmler's visit. From 1942 some worked as SS housekeepers; Gertrud Ott and 18 others at an SS hotel near Auschwitz passed literature to Ravensbrück; Gestapo raid 4 May 1944.
- **Source:** #2, #3, #7, #11 (V).
- **Game use:** Mother character's arc off-screen, told through letters; the "won't decorate for the birthday" refusal as a quiet act.

### 2.13 Death marches 1945
- **Fact:** Sachsenhausen evacuation: of 26,000 prisoners ~10,700 shot on the road to Schwerin. ~220 Witnesses covered ~200 km in two weeks, repaired shoes, pulled the weakest in **small carts** in turns; camped in a forest near Schwerin; shout "WE ARE FREE!"; waited for sunrise; count: 230 Witnesses from six nations + 36 others. Dachau march freed near Bad Tölz after a night in snow near Waakirchen.
- **Source:** #3 (V).
- **Game use:** Epilogue imagery: hand cart, forest at dawn.

### 2.14 Liberation and after
- **Fact:** Couldn't cross the Elbe for 2–3 months; mimeographed Watchtowers, preached in prison uniforms. Harald Abt liberated Buchenwald 11 Apr 1945 *during a Bible talk*; found daughter Jutta (~6) did not recognize parents (Aug 1945). Kusserow mother/Magdalena home Sep 1945; Karl-Heinz died of TB Oct 1946. Charlotte Müller home to Chemnitz Dec 1945; in GDR banned again 31 Aug 1950, re-arrested 1951 (8 years). Statistics (Yearbook): 6,019 arrested; ~2,000 to camps; 635 died in prison; 1,687 lost jobs; 735 lost homes; 860 children taken.
- **Source:** #3, #4, #6, #7 (V).
- **Game use:** Final card: a child who doesn't recognize a returning parent.

## 3. Person cards

### Kusserow family — Bad Lippspringe (Westphalia) — V (#4, #5), S (#18)
- **Family:** Franz and Hilda (baptized 1924); 11 children: Annemarie (eldest girl), Wilhelm, Siegfried (drowned age 20), Karl-Heinz, Wolfgang, Magdalena (7th, b. 1924), Waltraud, Hildegard, Hans-Werner, Elisabeth, Paul-Gerhard (youngest, b. 1931). Exact birth order beyond this: U.
- **Events:** 1931 moved to the Paderborn area to preach ~200 towns. House on ~3 acres with "Read 'The Golden Age'" signs; tram driver joked about the "Golden Age" stop; spring 1933 police ordered signs painted over — paint so thin the words showed through. Daily routine of chores, music, Bible teaching. ~18 Gestapo searches 1933–45; fugitives sheltered there. Father's pension cut for refusing "Heil Hitler". Spring 1939 three youngest taken from school (Dorsten → Nettelstadt). Wilhelm executed Münster (hospital garden, publicly) after Hitler rejected appeals; refused last chance to recant; father prayed at funeral in Bad Lippspringe. Wolfgang beheaded Brandenburg, age 20. Magdalena at 17: solitary in Vechta juvenile prison, refused release offer, ~3.5 yrs Ravensbrück with mother. Father imprisoned three times (incl. Kassel-Wehlheiden), home 1945 with broken leg, d. July 1950. 12 of 13 sentenced, total 65 years.
- **Paraphrased:** Wilhelm's last letter urged family to stay loyal to God; Magdalena recalled parents preparing children with practice answers.
- **Note:** USHMM conflict over which brother died in 1940 resolved by WT: Wilhelm 1940, Wolfgang 1942.

### Joseph (Josef) Hisiger — V (#8)
- b. 1 Mar 1914, Moselle (Lorraine). Catholic family. 1937 received an Elberfelder Bible from co-worker Albin Relewicz; Paris assembly Aug 1937; pioneer early 1939. Aug 1939 refused French military service → prison till July 1940. Under German annexation arrested after refusing "Heil Hitler" and party membership; beaten with revolver butt for refusing names; Sondergericht Metz 11 Sep 1942: 3 years. Zweibrücken labor camp (≈75 g bread mornings), Siegburg prison 1943 (<100 lb). Traded weekly bread for a Bible, read at night, carried under shirt. Two weeks solitary for passing a note. Freed April 1945 when guards fled. Cousin Jean executed as objector; Albin died Regensburg. Married Tina Minzani 13 Dec 1947.
- **Game use:** Bread-for-Bible trade.

### Harald (b. ~1912) and Elsa Abt — V (#6, #6b), S (#11)
- Harald entered Danzig Polytechnic 1931; met Elsa 1934; married June 1938; baptized early 1939; port engineer; Elsa smuggled magazines **under her clothes**. Fired Sept 1939 for refusing Hitler salute. Jutta b. 24 Sep 1939. Moved to Lodz Dec 1939. July 1940 Gestapo; Harald refused typed promise → Berlin prison → Sachsenhausen Sept 1940, no. 32,771; 1944 secret network uncovered; Buchenwald Oct 1944 (no. 76,667); liberated 11 Apr 1945. 14 years total in camps/prisons (incl. later). Elsa arrested May 1942 → Auschwitz-Birkenau, freed at Dora-Nordhausen area 1945 (brochure); toddler left with neighbors. Reunited; home Aug 1945; Jutta did not recognize them. Worked at Lodz branch 5 years.

### Charlotte Müller — V (#7, #11)
- b. 1912 Gotha-Siebleben (VIAF gives 1901 — conflict, prefer 1912). Family left church 2 Mar 1923; Chemnitz. Baptized March 1933. Distributed copied Watchtowers with sister Käthe. Arrested at home Aug 1936; Feb 1937 special court, 25 men + 2 women; she got 2 years; refused declaration → Lichtenburg (fire-hoses, dark cells) → Ravensbrück May 1939 (laundry; penal block ~1 yr) → SS housekeeper 1942 → fled west with family 1945 → Schwerin → Chemnitz Dec 1945 → Magdeburg Bethel 1946. GDR: arrested 1951, 8 years (Waldheim, Halle, Hoheneck), released Mar 1957, fled to West Berlin 6 May 1957.

### August Dickmann — V (#2, #10)
- b. 1910, Dinslaken. Had signed declaration after a prison term but later asked to annul it. Sachsenhausen from 1937 (arrest dates conflict — U). Refused induction slip Sept 1939; dungeon; public execution 15 Sep 1939, roll-call square; shot by three SS men + coup de grâce by senior officer (Yearbook). Brother Heinrich present.

### Martin Pötzinger — V (#9), S (#5)
- b. 25 Jul 1904 Munich; baptized 2 Oct 1928; pioneer 1 Oct 1930; Bulgaria 1933, deported; Hungary (arrested, deported); Yugoslavia, ill in Zagreb; married Gertrud 1936; arrested 1936 (Dec per one source — U); Dachau then Mauthausen; Gertrud in Ravensbrück (1943 cared for SS family children, Oranienburg — S). Reunited 1945 after 9 years. Later circuit work, Governing Body 1977; d. 16 Jun 1988 Brooklyn.

## 4. Everyday life 1933–1937, small Westphalian town (art/dialogue)
Status mostly **U/S** — general knowledge, not from WOL; verify before showing as explicit fact.
- **Greeting:** "Heil Hitler!" / Deutscher Gruß, obligatory for civil servants from July 1933 (U); refusal cost jobs/pensions — documented: Göhring fired, Abt fired, Franz Kusserow's pension cut (V #1, #4, #6). Regional everyday greeting: "Guten Tag", Catholic Paderborn area "Grüß Gott" less common than in the south (U).
- **Religion locally:** Paderborn region strongly Catholic — Witnesses a tiny visible minority (Kusserow: no local congregation in 1931, V #4).
- **Radio:** Volksempfänger VE 301 (number = 30 Jan, date of Hitler's appointment), launched Aug 1933, ~76 RM; DKE "Goebbels-Schnauze" 1938 ~35 RM (U). Radio announcing Magdeburg seizure 29 Jun 1933 (V #1).
- **Eintopfsonntag:** first 1 Oct 1933; one Sunday per month Oct–March; savings collected for Winterhilfswerk by party/organization collectors at the door (S #20). → natural door-knock scene.
- **Block warden (Blockleiter/"Blockwart"):** NSDAP lowest functionary for ~40–60 households; collected WHW donations, watched flags on holidays, reported "Meckerer" (U).
- **Flags:** households expected to fly the swastika on holidays (Hitler's birthday 20 Apr, 1 May); not flying it is noticeable (U; refusal to decorate documented in camps V #7).
- **Elections/plebiscites:** refusal to vote punished — Max Schubert paraded with a sign; Ludwig Stickel fired 1934 (V #1). Plebiscite dates: 12 Nov 1933, 19 Aug 1934, 29 Mar 1936 (U).
- **School:** morning flag ritual, Nazi songs, Hitler Youth / BDM membership pressure; Hitler Youth "Staatsjugendtag" Saturdays 1934–36 (U). Children of non-members threatened with removal (V #1).
- **Work:** Labor Front (DAF) dues compulsory — Gertrud Franke dismissed for objecting (V #1).
- **Interiors:** kitchen-living room with coal range (Kohleherd), pantry (Speisekammer), cellar with potato bin (V #2 for potato bin as hiding place), coffee pot, enamel jugs, crucifix/"Herrgottswinkel" in Catholic neighbors' homes, oilcloth table cover, wall clock (U).
- **Clothes:** men in flat caps, waistcoats, work jackets; women in aprons, headscarves, below-knee dresses; children in short trousers with knee socks, girls in braids (U).
- **Transport:** bicycle as main courier vehicle (V #1), trams (Kusserow house tram stop, V #4), motorcycles rare.
- **Literature objects:** *Das Goldene Zeitalter* (The Golden Age) sign on house (V #4); *Wachtturm* stencils; *Daily Heavenly Manna* birthday book (dangerous—used by Gestapo for addresses, V #2).
- **Posters:** "Ein Volk, ein Reich, ein Führer" (from 1938 — avoid for 1933–37), WHW collection posters, Stürmer display cases (Stürmerkasten) in towns (U).

## 5. Narrative raw material — playable situations
1. **The thin paint (1933):** Police order the family to paint over a religious sign; the old words keep showing through the whitewash. (Kusserow, V)
2. **The knock:** Search during dinner; mother slips literature into the coffee pot and hands it to an aunt; the youngest walks out with a schoolbag and waits by the gate. (Kornelius, V)
3. **Potato bin:** Night copying on a duplicator; at dawn it must go under the potatoes before the milkman passes. (Brembach, V)
4. **Card game cover:** A meeting turns into a card game when an unexpected visitor arrives. (Kohlhofer, V)
5. **12 Dec 1936, 17:00–19:00:** Winter dark, a bag of ~30 leaflets, one per letterbox, three for tenement houses; police patrols; return before 19:00. (V)
6. **Morning after:** A policeman asks the neighbor whether she found a leaflet; she says no and closes her door. Player never learns whether she read it. (V pattern)
7. **The flag ritual:** Child stands silent while classmates salute; teacher choice of mockery vs. letting it pass. (Kusserow, Knöller, Seitz, V)
8. **Taken from school:** Police arrive at the classroom with a court order for three siblings. (Kusserow 1939, V)
9. **The birthday book:** Father realizes the family's devotional birthday book lists names and addresses — burn it or keep it? (V)
10. **The Erklärung:** A five-point form on the table; the clause "report anyone who approaches you" is the turning point. Variant: shortened version for a nursing mother. (V)
11. **Stew-pot pickup:** A routine handoff at a usual meeting point; something feels wrong. (Löhr, V)
12. **The bluff:** Interrogator claims a friend already named you. (Schneider, V)
13. **Eintopf Sunday collector:** The block collector at the door asks for the WHW donation and glances at the missing flag. (setting U)
14. **Five lines a month:** Writing a letter from detention with a strict line limit, censored. (V)
15. **Bread for a Bible:** A prisoner trades a week's bread for a smuggled Bible. (Hisiger, V)
